TWO WAYS TO SAVE A STORY

DIGITAL

A digital copy can make words and images accessible to readers far beyond the original publication.

Digital preservation can improve discoverability, remote access, searching, and sharing. It also requires continued stewardship of formats and files.

PHYSICAL

The original carries evidence of paper, ink, binding, scale, wear, and how the publication was used.

The physical publication preserves material and design evidence that a scan cannot fully convey. Keeping both forms offers a fuller historical record.

06 / WHAT PAPER HOLDS

Reading on paper has a comprehension advantage.

A meta-analysis of 54 studies found a small average advantage in reading comprehension for physical print over digital screens.

A small average advantage for print

HEDGES’ G

The positive pooled effect indicates a small average advantage. It is not a 21% increase.

Hedges’ g = 0.21. A meta-analysis of 54 studies found a small average reading-comprehension advantage for paper over screens. This is a standardized effect size, not a percentage. It does not describe every reader or every type of reading.
Source: Delgado et al. (2018), “Don't throw away your printed books.”
07 / THE ASK

Keep the pages. Keep the evidence.

Libraries, archives, and cultural institutions should preserve physical publications alongside their digital counterparts, so future generations can experience and study the full cultural record.

PRESERVATION IN PRACTICE

The Mellon Foundation

The Mellon Foundation supports efforts to preserve and expand access to cultural knowledge. Its work has helped libraries establish community memory labs, where people can digitize and safeguard personal media such as photographs, VHS tapes, and other analog collections.

This example highlights how digital access can complement the preservation of material history.
